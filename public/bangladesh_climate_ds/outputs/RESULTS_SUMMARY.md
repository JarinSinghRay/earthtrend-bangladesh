# Bangladesh climate trends 2001-2025 - results summary

*Source: NASA POWER Regional Monthly (MERRA-2 reanalysis): T2M, T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN (°C), PRECTOTCORR (mm/day). 54 grid cells cover Bangladesh.*

## Headline findings

- TEMPERATURE: Mean temperature shows no statistically significant trend: Sen's slope +0.03 °C/decade (95% CI -0.13 to +0.18; p = 0.761).
- DAY vs NIGHT: Maximum temperature shows no statistically significant trend: Sen's slope -0.15 °C/decade (95% CI -0.38 to +0.18; p = 0.293). Minimum temperature shows only a weak, non-significant tendency to rise: Sen's slope +0.14 °C/decade (95% CI -0.05 to +0.30; p = 0.088).
- RAINFALL (data-quality alert): national annual rainfall in this product jumps from 1843 mm/yr (up to 2014) to 3136 mm/yr (from 2015), a x1.70 step (Pettitt p = 0.0003) seen in every month and every grid cell. The apparent +628 mm/decade 'trend' is therefore mostly this step; within the sub-periods no significant trend remains. Verify with an independent product (GPM IMERG / CHIRPS / BMD gauges) before treating it as real climate change.
- SEASONS: the largest national mean-temperature rise is in the Post-monsoon season (+0.32 °C/decade, p = 0.023); 23 of 54 grid cells are significant after FDR correction. At national level this is significant before but not after FDR correction across all national tests - treat as an emerging signal.
- WHERE: annual mean temperature has 34 of 54 cells with positive slope; fastest in Chattogram area (+0.19 °C/decade at 22.5N, 91.25E), 0 cells significant after FDR - i.e. no cell has a statistically reliable annual warming trend, so spatial differences in annual warming should be read as tendencies, not facts. Highest regional mean warming rate: Sylhet.
- EXTREMES: hottest year 2016 (25.97 °C), coolest 2020 (24.88 °C); hottest month anomaly 2005-06 (z = 2.8); wettest rated month 2017-03 (z = 3.4).
- SEASONALITY: May is the warmest month (29.7 °C), January the coolest; monsoon (Jun-Sep) brings 69% of annual rainfall, peaking in July.
- CORRELATION: annual maximum temperature vs rainfall r = -0.31 (p = 0.133, weak negative; yearly values, step change removed (series demeaned before/after 2015)).
- PATTERN: in the Pre-monsoon season, hotter years are drier: Tmax vs rainfall r = -0.54 (p = 0.006, n = 25) even after removing the rainfall step change - consistent with cloud/rain cooling of daytime temperature.

## The 5 scientific questions

### 1. What is changing?

- Mean temperature shows no statistically significant trend: Sen's slope +0.03 °C/decade (95% CI -0.13 to +0.18; p = 0.761).
- Maximum temperature shows no statistically significant trend: Sen's slope -0.15 °C/decade (95% CI -0.38 to +0.18; p = 0.293).
- Minimum temperature shows only a weak, non-significant tendency to rise: Sen's slope +0.14 °C/decade (95% CI -0.05 to +0.30; p = 0.088).
- Diurnal temperature range (DTR) decreased: Sen's slope -0.31 °C/decade (95% CI -0.63 to -0.04; p = 0.030) - significant before, but not after, FDR correction across the national tests; a step change around 2015 was detected (Pettitt p = 0.008), so this is not a smooth trend.
- Rainfall increased: Sen's slope +628 mm per year/decade (95% CI +384 to +943; p < 0.001); a step change around 2015 was detected (Pettitt p < 0.001), so this is not a smooth trend.

### 4. Is it statistically significant?

- Trend is called significant when p < 0.05 (Mann-Kendall; TFPW pre-whitening if lag-1 autocorrelation was significant). For many grid cells, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q < 0.05) is used so that false discoveries are controlled.
- National tests: 8 of 25 significant (raw), 3 after FDR.
- Grid cells, t2m/Annual: 2/54 significant (raw), 0/54 after FDR.
- Grid cells, tmax/Annual: 3/54 significant (raw), 0/54 after FDR.
- Grid cells, tmin/Annual: 18/54 significant (raw), 8/54 after FDR.
- Grid cells, rain/Annual: 52/54 significant (raw), 52/54 after FDR.
- Grid cells, t2m/Monsoon: 26/54 significant (raw), 21/54 after FDR.
- Grid cells, t2m/Post-monsoon: 32/54 significant (raw), 23/54 after FDR.

### 5. Important climate patterns

- Warmest month May (29.7 °C); coolest January (17.5 °C).
- Monsoon (Jun-Sep) supplies 69% of annual rainfall.
- Diurnal temperature range decreased: Sen's slope -0.31 °C/decade (95% CI -0.63 to -0.04; p = 0.030) - significant before, but not after, FDR correction across the national tests; a step change around 2015 was detected (Pettitt p = 0.008), so this is not a smooth trend.

## Caveats

- MERRA-2 is a reanalysis (model + assimilated observations), not station data; trends can include changes in the observing system.
- 25 years is short for climate-trend work; interannual variability (e.g. ENSO, monsoon strength) is large relative to the signal.
- Grid cells are 0.5°x0.625° (~55x65 km); Bangladesh cells are selected with a simplified boundary outline (>= 20% cell overlap).
- Neighbouring cells are spatially correlated, so FDR (which assumes weak dependence) is approximate; interpret clusters of significant cells together.
- Rainfall shows a step change in this product (see step_change_check); compare with an independent rainfall dataset before drawing climate conclusions.
- Correlation does not imply causation.
